Nobody Move, Nobody Gets Hurt

Revealing the fear that the victim will become aware of how precarious this apparent power imbalance is.
August 13, 2025 · 1 min · 194 words · J. McMullen

This is everyone’s favorite part of the bank job. Our guys - we’re usually rooting for the robbers - are letting everyone know that they have no intention of committing wanton violence; they’re here for the bank’s money, not your money. You have no reason to be a hero, it will gain you nothing and we’ll be forced to maintain control whatever means necessary. If you do not cooperate, people will get hurt, it will be your fault, and we’ll all be back where we started but with more bumps, bruises, or holes in us. The likelihood of experiencing this particular situation is low. I would love to watch a gang of tactically competent, smooth-ass criminals walk out of a bank with bags of money. What is more common is the tacit threat from an abusor, an oppressor, or an authority. “Behave.” “Don’t make me hurt you.” “Don’t force me to respond.” “Do not make waves.” “Do what you’re told or there will be consequences.” More palatably, “everything is fine, just sit still. We’ve got this covered.”

There’s a video of Sinead doing “The Emporer’s New Clothes” from 1990 you can find on YouTube.
