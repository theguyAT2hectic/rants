Guilty Pleasures

We cannot control that which we hold dear
August 13, 2025 · 3 min · J. McMullen

The blanket acceptance of using the term ‘guilty pleasure’ to excuse our indulgence of things that aren’t collectively considered cool, classy, or otherwise justifiable is an efficient indicator of how necessary these transgressions of taste are. The longer you’re alive, or, rather, the more experiences you cobble together, the less control you have over the things that hold meaning for you and, thus, become indulgences. Only from the safe, sterile perch of the critic’s desk can one maintain a proper set of standards for art and food and music or anything else. As I get older, though, I am beginning to wonder if these guilty ones have the most meat. They end up being the little splashes of color that decorate your brutalist, non-descript cubicle that your life can become when you’re not paying attention. IF* you’re thoughtful, you start to find that your tastes are arguably more defined than the so-called critic’s - that lived experience trumps the academic every time. The silliness of critique is well-illustrated by Mr. Keating in his first interaction with students in Dead Poets Society. Young Neil Perry reads from the introduction to their poetry text as written by Dr. J. Evan Pritchard, PhD. He explains that we can evaluate a poem by scoring it on a Cartesian plane with Perfection on the $x$-axis and Importance on the $y$. We can thus determine the poem’s greatness by it’s total area. Mr. Keating calls this “excrement” and orders these buttoned-up little foundlings to tear out the page &mdash no, tear out the whole introduction &mdash from their pristine text books which they are likely to be fined for come the end of the semester. The lesson here is more layered than we give it credit for. This is a lesson in interrogating the sacred. Both the completeness of the book and the authority of the voice therein are categorically dismissed. Such a lesson is a risky one to give to young men (I can only speak as having been a young man though I expect it is not limited by sex or gender). Now, authority is going to come under question eventually. At least one of a group will be stricken awake one late night when they say to themselves wait a second, why are we doing this? why is that even a thing? I specify the one because it isn’t always clear that you’re abiding by totally arbitrary rules and conventions and there is little likelihood that someone is going to point it out to you. Even in middle age, I find myself wandering around just doing stuff I’m supposed to. A couple of years ago, I was (uncharacteristically) heling my wife make the bed. I asked her how she knew which direction the fitted sheet went on the bed. She said there was a little tag in one of the corners and that she put that one in the top left. Exasperated by the notion that it involved investigation at all, I quipped “Why don’t we just take a marker and little stick figures of us lying in the bed &mdash I mean they’re our fucking sheets.” We laughed a little more than normal people would but, yes, they ARE our goddamn sheets and we can do whatever we want to them.
